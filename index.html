Seven tabs

Everything you reach for.
One click away.

Show the tabs you use, hide the rest, and drag them into your own order.

Note

A blank page for whatever just came to mind. Open it, type, close it.

  • Saves as you type, nothing to lose
  • Pick the font, size and weight that feel right
  • Right where you left it, every time

Work

A daily log of what you worked on, ready for your weekly update.

  • Tasks with a link, % done and time spent
  • Week view with totals; step back through past weeks
  • Copy the week as text for Slack or email
  • Reminders at as many times a day as you like

Deploys

Vercel deployments and GitHub Actions runs, side by side.

  • Notifications when a build starts, finishes or fails
  • Pulsing tray dot while anything is building
  • Filter by Ready, Building, Error or Canceled
  • Redeploy or re-run failed jobs in one click
  • Multiple accounts, tokens kept in the Keychain

Ports

Every server listening on your Mac, and what started it.

  • Local and network URLs, ready to copy or open
  • Test on your phone over Wi-Fi with the network URL
  • Copy the project path, or stop the process
  • Refreshes every 10 seconds while open

AI

How much of your Claude Code and Codex limits you've used.

  • 5-hour and weekly windows with reset times
  • Uses your existing CLI sign-in, read-only
  • Backs off politely if the provider rate-limits

Health

A quick check-up for your Mac, without opening three apps.

  • Battery charge, health and cycle count
  • CPU, memory pressure and swap
  • Free disk space and thermal state
  • Power draw and the apps using the most energy

Clipboard

The last 300 things you copied, text and images.

  • Search, then click any item to copy it again
  • Pin favourites and give them labels
  • Draw on any copied image with the pen (saves a copy)
  • Compact view, with a big preview when you hover an image
  • Handles huge copies, like a full JSON response

Screenshots

Capture it. Read it.
Mark it up.

The camera button beside the pin. Every shot is copied straight away, so it's ready to paste.

Capture

An area or the whole screen, with the crosshair you know from ⌘⇧4.

  • Capture area or capture screen
  • Delayed area: select it, it's captured 3 seconds later - time to open that menu
  • Delayed screen: the whole screen after 3 seconds
  • A preview pops up in the middle of the screen: copy, save to Desktop or open

Read text

Select any part of the screen and get its text - from images, videos, PDFs or apps that won't let you copy.

  • Recognised on your Mac, nothing is uploaded
  • The text is copied for you, and shown to check

Mark it up

A simple pen to circle the bug or underline the number before you send it.

  • Seven colours, three sizes, undo
  • Works on screenshots and on images in your clipboard history
  • Done saves and copies the marked-up image

Screenshots and text recognition are on macOS for now.

Setup

Up and running in 2 minutes

01

Install Nook

macOS: download the DMG for your Mac below and drag Nook to Applications. Nook isn't notarized by Apple yet, so run this once in Terminal before opening it:

$ xattr -d com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Nook.app

If it fails, put sudo in front and try again.

Linux: the AppImage runs on most distros and updates itself; the .deb installs on Ubuntu and Debian.

$ chmod +x Nook_amd64.AppImage && ./Nook_amd64.AppImage
$ sudo apt install ./Nook_amd64.deb
02

Click the icon in your menu bar

Nook lives next to the clock. Click it to open, click away to hide. Right-click (two-finger click) for updates and Quit.

On Linux, click the tray icon and choose Open Nook - or turn on the floating button in Settings to open it with one click. Vanilla GNOME (e.g. Fedora) needs the AppIndicator extension to show tray icons; Ubuntu and KDE have it built in.

03

Connect deploys (optional)

Create a Vercel token and a GitHub token with repo and workflow scopes, then add them in the Deploys tab. They're stored in your macOS Keychain, or your keyring on Linux.

04

Make it yours

Open Settings to hide or reorder tabs, switch to vertical tabs, turn on the floating button and notifications. The AI tab picks up Claude Code and Codex on its own if you're signed in to their CLIs. The first screenshot asks for Screen Recording permission.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is Nook free?

Yes. No account, no trial, no subscription.

Which systems are supported?

macOS 11 (Big Sur) or later, on Apple Silicon and Intel.

Linux on x86_64 with a system tray: Ubuntu 22.04 or newer, Debian 12, Fedora, and similar distros, on GNOME or KDE, X11 or Wayland.

Where is my data stored?

On your computer. Notes, the work log and clipboard history stay in Nook's local app data. Vercel and GitHub tokens go in the macOS Keychain, or the system keyring on Linux.

Screenshots are kept in Nook's cache for a day unless you save them, and text recognition runs on your Mac - nothing is uploaded.

Nook only talks to Vercel, GitHub and the Claude/Codex usage endpoints you've set up, plus its own update check.

What does the clipboard history save?

The last 300 text and image copies, plus anything you pin. Copies that apps mark as temporary are skipped; everything else is kept, so delete anything sensitive with the trash icon, or clear the history.

Will it drain my battery?

No. Deploys are checked every 15 seconds only while something is building, and every 2 minutes when the window is closed. Animations stop while the window is hidden, and on macOS everything pauses while your screen is locked or the display is asleep.

Why does macOS say Nook can't be opened?

Nook isn't notarized by Apple yet. Move it to Applications, then run:

$ xattr -d com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Nook.app
How do updates work?

Nook checks for new versions on its own. When one is out, right-click the menu bar icon or open Settings and choose Install. It restarts in a few seconds.

On Linux the AppImage updates the same way. The .deb doesn't: install the new .deb from this page when an update is out.
